The problem

You're using AI.
So is everyone else.

Most organisations are in the honeymoon period. Things are moving faster, ideas are easier to articulate, output is up; it feels productive, and often it is. But there's more happening beneath the surface and almost everyone is struggling with the following questions:

Org design

  • How do you maintain coherence — consistent connection of parts — when people and teams are moving faster and faster?
  • Management just got a whole lot more complex, but the potential of teams just got a lot bigger. How can you help realise this?

People

  • It's not all bliss and breakthroughs — you're seeing people with the dark side of bot addiction. Communication is breaking down, innovation is stalling, the best and brightest are leaving to pursue meaning and layoffs are mounting. Culture becomes brittle.

Path dependency // Strategy

  • Is this the end of Big SAAS? In a world of infinite, cheap replication, who will be the winners and losers and why? How do you position yourself? How do you decide what to build yourselves and what to outsource amongst shifting data prices, cost structures, and complex horizons?
In a landscape being rapidly flattened by AI, humanness is the moat and coherence is the bridge

The organisations that navigate hyperintelligence well will do so because they deepened the specifically human capacities that no technology can replicate.

Approach

We work alongside organisations holding two problems at once: something is not working in how their people relate, decide, and act together — and they are adopting new AI tooling fast. These are not separate problems. We work across two tracks simultaneously.

01

Organisational attunement

The culture, governance, and design work that determines whether AI lands in fertile or fractured ground. Executive coaching, leadership team work, organisation design, and facilitation of your hardest meetings and conversations.

02

AI adoption strategy

Which tools to trust, which capabilities to build, which dependencies to avoid, and where human judgment must remain sovereign. Not generic consulting — strategy that works with the technical and the human together.
